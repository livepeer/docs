Understanding gateway economics starts with a key fact: **gateways are payers, not earners, at the protocol level.** The protocol routes ETH from gateways to orchestrators. There is no built-in protocol reward for gateways — that's not the model.

Gateway operators earn by running a service business on top of the network: charging applications more for compute access than the underlying orchestrator cost. The margin is the business.

## How payment tickets work

Gateways do not pay for every segment individually on-chain — that would make Arbitrum gas costs prohibitive. Instead they use a **probabilistic micropayment system**:

1. For each video segment or AI inference job completed, the gateway creates a signed payment ticket with a face value and a win probability.
2. The ticket is sent off-chain to the orchestrator alongside the job result.
3. Most tickets don't win. A small percentage — determined by the win probability — are redeemable on-chain for the face value.
4. The orchestrator redeems winning tickets by calling `RedeemWinningTicket()` on Arbitrum.
5. Funds are transferred from the gateway's reserve to the orchestrator's account.

The expected value of the ticket stream equals fair payment for the work done. The orchestrator's risk is spread across many tickets, and the gateway's cost is predictable at volume.

## How gateway operators monetise

Gateways make money through **margin on compute**: charging applications more for access to Livepeer compute than the orchestrator rate, and keeping the difference.

The protocol does not enforce any particular business model. How you price access to your gateway is entirely up to you — the protocol only controls what you pay orchestrators.

## Withdrawal and exit

To recover your ETH deposit and reserve:

1. Call `Unlock()` on the TicketBroker contract to start the withdrawal countdown
2. Wait for the unlock period to expire (prevents mid-session fund removal)
3. Call `Withdraw()` to receive your remaining deposit and reserve

During the unlock period, your gateway can still operate. Orchestrators may become more conservative about accepting sessions from an unlocking gateway.
