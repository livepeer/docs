Gateways occupy the demand side of the Livepeer network. They are the control point between applications and the GPU compute network — handling routing, payments, service-level logic, and customer relationships.

This page is for anyone evaluating whether running a gateway is the right strategic or business decision.

## The Core Business Model

Gateways in Livepeer do not earn fees at the protocol level. Orchestrators earn protocol fees; gateways earn at the **business layer** — the margin between what you charge customers and what you pay orchestrators.

```
Customer pays Gateway → Gateway pays Orchestrator → Gateway keeps the margin
```

This model is similar to how cloud providers, CDNs, and API intermediaries work: the compute is commoditised and distributed; the value is in reliability, customer relationships, product surface, and trust.

## Why Operate a Gateway

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1. Direct Usage & Product Integration">
    Run your own workloads through your own gateway for full control over ingestion, routing, retries, and delivery. No third-party gateway fees, no policy constraints.

    - Process your own video or AI content end-to-end
    - Enforce SLAs on orchestrators: latency, availability, failover
    - Use the gateway as internal infrastructure powering a larger product

    **Example:** A live streaming platform routes all encoder traffic through its own gateway, giving it full control over which orchestrators handle its streams and at what quality targets.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="2. Service Monetization">
    Charge customers above orchestrator cost — for reliability, compliance, custom features, or convenience.

    - Set your own pricing independent of protocol economics
    - Bundle gateway access with value-added services (dashboards, analytics, support)
    - Avoid paying third-party gateway margins on your own traffic

    **Example:** A video API product charges $0.005/min for transcoding, pays $0.003/min to orchestrators, and keeps $0.002/min as service margin.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="3. Demand Ownership & Traffic Control">
    Own ingress, customer relationships, usage data, and traffic predictability.

    - Aggregate demand from multiple apps or customers through one routing layer
    - Normalize bursty demand into predictable, orchestrator-friendly workloads
    - Maintain complete visibility into usage patterns and failure modes

    **Example:** An AI studio routes 50+ client applications through its gateway, maintaining a unified view of GPU usage, costs, and quality across all customers.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="4. Reliability & Performance Control">
    Define and enforce your own quality of service, independent of protocol defaults.

    - Control routing, retries, failover, and latency/cost tradeoffs
    - Route users to geographically optimal orchestrators
    - Build custom health-check logic for your SLA requirements

    **Example:** A real-time AI video product needs sub-500ms inference latency. It routes exclusively to orchestrators in specific regions and maintains a warm pool to avoid cold-start penalties.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="5. Enterprise & Compliance Requirements">
    Large customers require policy controls that only exist at the gateway layer.

    - IP allowlists, audit logs, authentication, and rate limits
    - Data residency and geographic routing constraints
    - Cost-explosion protection against buggy or malicious clients

    **Example:** An enterprise media company requires all video processing to route through EU-hosted orchestrators only. This policy is enforced at the gateway, not the protocol.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="6. Product Differentiation">
    Build differentiated products above the protocol — stable APIs, custom AI workflow presets, SDKs, dashboards, and billing abstractions.

    - Shield customers from protocol or orchestrator churn with versioning
    - Build features that differentiate your offering from other gateways
    - Create proprietary AI pipeline configurations available only through your gateway

    **Example:** Livepeer Studio and Daydream are both gateway products — each differentiated by its unique feature set and target market, built on the same underlying network.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="7. Ecosystem Influence & Optionality">
    Gateways shape real-world usage patterns, surface protocol gaps, and influence the direction of the network.

    - Position early as protocol economics and gateway incentives evolve
    - Operate at the layer where business logic, pricing, and customer trust live
    - Contribute to ecosystem standards and best practices

    **Example:** Cloud SPE operates as a public-good gateway, funded by the Livepeer treasury, improving ecosystem adoption and tooling for all operators.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The Gateway Opportunity Space

As Livepeer's AI inference network grows, gateway operators are positioned at the layer where:

- **Demand aggregation** happens — applications connect to gateways, not directly to orchestrators
- **Service differentiation** is built — feature sets, pricing models, and SLAs are gateway-layer decisions
- **Business relationships** live — customers buy from gateway operators, not from the protocol

The underlying GPU supply is decentralized and competitive. The opportunity is in building the product, service, and customer layer on top of it.

<BorderedBox variant="accent" padding="16px">

**Think of it this way:**
Orchestrators are factories. Gateways are the logistics companies, distributors, and branded products built on top. In mature infrastructure markets, the branded, customer-facing layer captures durable value even when the underlying execution is commoditised.

</BorderedBox>
